# Kaizen OS waitlist launch plan

## Purpose

This waitlist should answer one clear question: **is there enough qualified demand for Kaizen OS to justify further product investment?** The first launch should therefore prioritize conversations with people who resemble the intended users over broad, unqualified traffic.

## Before announcing the waitlist

The public landing page, Google Sheet collector, duplicate guard, and welcome email should be confirmed with one final real submission after each production update. The site should use the GitHub Pages URL, and the owner should check that the lead appears in the `Waitlist` tab, the confirmation status changes to `Yes`, and the welcome email arrives.

Create a simple weekly operating rhythm around the Google Sheet. Review the **Dashboard** once a week, read the optional company and role fields, and tag promising leads by audience type in the `Notes` column. Do not treat raw sign-up volume as the only signal. A smaller number of people who closely match the product’s intended customer profile is more useful than a large number of casual sign-ups.

| Readiness item | Owner action | Completion signal |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Site QA | Submit a new controlled email through the public URL. | One row, one welcome email, and a successful dashboard update. |
| Inbox readiness | Check the sending Gmail account and reply path. | The welcome email has correct branding and reply contact details. |
| Lead review | Agree on what makes a lead promising. | A short note format is ready for `Notes`, such as audience, pain point, and follow-up priority. |
| Measurement | Define the weekly review time and decision threshold. | The team can state what level of qualified interest would justify the next prototype or pilot. |

## Launch the first small cohort

Begin with a personal, founder-led launch rather than paid promotion. Send a short individual email or LinkedIn message to 20–30 people who may experience the operating problems Kaizen OS intends to solve. Explain the product premise in one sentence, ask for their honest view, and invite them to the waitlist only if the premise is relevant.

Use the existing product gallery as the visual proof point. The launch message should frame the screenshots as an evolving early concept, not a finished product. This keeps the invitation honest and encourages useful feedback rather than feature requests based on a promise of immediate access.

A practical first post can use this structure:

> Kaizen OS is a small experiment in making focused work feel less noisy: one clear next step, a repeatable rhythm, and quieter progress signals. We are testing whether this solves a real problem for ambitious teams. If that sounds familiar, join the early waitlist and tell us what currently gets in the way.

Post the same core positioning through the PhoennixAI website and LinkedIn presence first. Use X and Instagram primarily to point people to the visual product story rather than expecting them to produce the earliest qualified leads.

## Run a two-week learning loop

During the first two weeks, share the link in only a few places at a time. For each channel, use a distinct campaign link so you can identify which audience produces useful sign-ups. A future small enhancement can record campaign parameters from the URL into the Sheet; until then, keep a simple source log beside the campaign posts.

Review new leads twice a week. Reply personally to the most relevant people with one short question: *What is the hardest part of maintaining focus or operating rhythm in your work today?* Record the answer in `Notes`. This turns the waitlist into a research channel rather than a passive email list.

| Signal | Why it matters | Suggested early interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Qualified leads | Shows relevance to the intended audience. | More important than total sign-ups. |
| Company and role patterns | Reveals where the proposition is resonating. | Look for repeated job contexts or team types. |
| Optional update opt-in | Indicates permission for future product-news communication. | Use only for optional news, not operational messages. |
| Replies and conversations | Reveals urgency and language users use themselves. | Capture recurring problems and outcomes. |
| Duplicate submissions | Confirms the collector is safe against repeat clicks. | It should remain a no-op rather than create a second row. |

## Decide what to do after the test

At the end of two weeks, group leads into three categories: **strong fit**, **possible fit**, and **low-fit or unclear**. Review the notes, role patterns, and conversations. If a coherent group repeatedly describes the same operating problem, invite a small set of strong-fit leads to 20-minute discovery conversations before building more product.

If the interest is broad but vague, refine the landing-page positioning and repeat the test with a more specific target segment. If the interest is both low and unqualified, treat that as useful evidence: pause further promotion and reconsider the product premise before investing in a larger build.

## Compliance and trust

Use the welcome email only to confirm the waitlist registration and explain next steps. Send promotional news only to people who checked the optional updates box. Keep the privacy notice and contact route current, and honor removal requests promptly through PhoennixAI.

## Immediate sequence

1. Verify the repaired public site with one new unique email address.
2. Announce the waitlist privately to a first cohort of 20–30 relevant people.
3. Publish one concise PhoennixAI/LinkedIn post using the product gallery screenshots.
4. Review the Dashboard and lead quality twice a week for two weeks.
5. Invite the strongest early leads into short discovery conversations before deciding on a broader launch.
